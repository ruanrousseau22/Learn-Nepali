First words

The ones that open every door

Greet BEFORE anything — a request without molo lands rudely. Nearly everyone also speaks English; your isiXhosa buys warmth, not survival, and even clumsy clicks earn real delight.
MAW-lawhello (to one person)molo
mawl-WEH-neehello (to several people)molweni
oon-JAH-neehow are you?Unjani?
ndee-yah-PEE-lahI am finendiyaphila
ndee-KHAW-nahI am okayndikhona
ehn-KAW-seethank youenkosi
oo-XAW-lawsorry · excuse meuxolo

Everyone is family

Address people and doors open

Elders are mama and tata, peers are bhuti and sisi — the address IS the politeness system. There is no everyday please-word; respect rides on how you call someone.
MAW-law MAH-mahhello, madamMolo mama
MAW-law TAH-tahhello, sirMolo tata
ehn-KAW-see SEE-seethank you, sisEnkosi sisi
oo-XAW-law BHOO-teeexcuse me, broUxolo bhuti
BHOO-teebro (any young man)bhuti
SEE-seesis (any young woman)sisi

Meeting someone

The first minute, in isiXhosa

Your name and where you are from will be asked within a minute — answer and ask back. It is connection, not interrogation, and it usually ends in an invitation.
ee-GAH-mah LAH-kaw ngoo-BAH-neewhat is your name?Igama lakho ngubani?
oon-goo-BAH-neewho are you?Ungubani?
ndee-yah-VOO-yah oo-koo-KWAH-zeenice to meet youNdiyavuya ukukwazi
oo-VEH-lah PEEwhere are you from?Uvela phi?
ndee-VEH-lah peh-SHEH-yahI am from overseasNdivela phesheya
oo-TEH-tah ee-see-XHAW-sahdo you speak Xhosa?Uthetha isiXhosa?
ndee-sah-FOON-dahI am still learningNdisafunda

What you will hear

Meant kindly — answer warmly

Uya phi? from a stranger on the road is friendliness, not nosiness. Wamkelekile — you are welcome — is what every host says at the door, and means it.
OO-yah PEEwhere are you going? (a greeting)Uya phi?
koon-JAH-neehow is it going?Kunjani?
wahm-keh-leh-KEE-lehyou are welcome hereWamkelekile
YEE-zah AH-pahcome hereYiza apha
oo-TEH-tah oo-koo-TEE-neewhat do you mean?Uthetha ukuthini?
HAHM-bah kah-KOO-hlehgo well (said to you, leaving)hamba kakuhle
SAH-lah kah-KOO-hlehstay well (you say it, leaving)sala kakuhle

The minibus taxi

How South Africa moves

Hail with a hand sign for your route, pay by passing coins forward, and call your stop — Yima! or the English Short left! Every route begins at the rank.
ee-TEHK-seethe minibus taxiiteksi
ee-REHN-keethe taxi rankirenki

ndee-HAHM-bah ngeh-TEHK-seeI go by taxiNdihamba ngeteksi

Eating & the braai

Where the language tastes best

A shisanyama is a braai spot — buy meat, they grill it, the tables are shared. Kumnandi! (delicious!) is the highest compliment a cook can hear.

Being a guest

You will be fed — accept

Guests are fed, full stop — refusing everything reads as rejection. Eat something, praise it, and say Ndihluthi enkosi only when you truly cannot go on.

ndee-HLOO-tee ehn-KAW-seeI am full, thank youNdihluthi enkosi

The spaza & the till

Corner-shop isiXhosa

The spaza is the home-run corner shop, open when nothing else is. Prices are fixed there and at tills — bargain at market stalls only, with a smile.

Staying in touch

Friends, not contacts

Sobonana — we will see each other — is the goodbye between friends. Swap numbers, send a voice note in isiXhosa, and you have made a friend, not a contact.

Common questions

How do you say hello in Xhosa?

Do I need an account to use these Xhosa phrases?

No. Every phrase on this page is open to read, and you can hear all of them in Bhasaly without signing up.

Do I need to read the Xhosa script to use these?

No. Every line is romanized first, so you can say it straight away. The script is there alongside if you want to start reading it.
