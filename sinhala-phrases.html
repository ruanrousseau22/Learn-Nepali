First words

The go-and-come-back goodbye

aayuboowan opens formally — may you live long. The goodbye is a round trip: gihin ennam, I will go and come back, answered gihin enna. kamak naee — no problem — smooths everything over.

samaawennasorry · excuse meසමාවෙන්න
ovyesඔව්
naeenoනෑ

Being polite

ayiyaa and akkaa

ayiyaa and akkaa — older brother, older sister — address waiters, drivers and shopkeepers warmly. mata epaa declines anything without offence.
ayiyaaolder brother — any man a bit olderඅයියා
akkaaolder sister — any woman a bit olderඅක්කා
malliiyounger brotherමල්ලී
nangiiyounger sisterනංගී

Tea arrives in minutes

bath kaaladha? — eaten rice? — is a greeting; kaalaa answers it. thee bonna enna — come drink tea — is meant literally, and soon.

The three-wheeler

Meters in Colombo, deals upcountry

The tuk-tuk is the thriiwiil eka. In Colombo insist on the meter or fix the price first; upcountry it is all negotiation. Buses stop for a raised hand.
thriiwiil ekathe three-wheeler (tuk-tuk)ත්‍රීවීල් එක

Rice & curry

Fiery, by hand, wonderful

Rice and curry is lunch, eaten by hand, and it can be FIERY — harima saerayi! earns sympathy and wathura tikak puts the fire out. The orange thaembili is the roadside drink.

Shopping

The smiling negotiation

ganan waediyi plus adu karanna is the whole negotiation, done with a smile. Supermarkets are fixed; the street market bends a little.

Staying somewhere

Mind the false friend

A hootalee is often an eatery, not lodging — places to sleep say guest house. dhawasakata kiiyadha asks the day rate; a fan often beats the aircon price.

Getting help

Serious words, hopefully unused

udhaw karanna! — please help — is the sentence to know; say it clearly. praweesamen is what everyone will call after you as you leave.

Staying in touch

Phone, wifi & photos

Guesthouse wifi is standard; a local SIM needs your passport. Ask foto ekak ganna puluwandha before photographing people.
