First words

The ones that open Ethiopia

Selam works any hour; tena yistilign is the formal version. English is thin outside hotels — these words are survival, and every attempt at Amharic is met with delight.

tena yistilignformal greeting, any hourጤና ይስጥልኝ

He and she, always

Amharic greets by gender

Every you-word splits: -h to a man, -sh to a woman. Elders get erswo forms. Getting the split right is basic courtesy, and locals warmly correct you when you miss.
dehna neh?are you well? (to a man)ደህና ነህ?
dehna nesh?are you well? (to a woman)ደህና ነሽ?
dehna newot?are you well? (polite, elders)ደህና ነዎት?
dehna negnI am wellደህና ነኝ
ibakihplease (to a man)እባክህ
ibakishplease (to a woman)እባክሽ
gashwarm address for an older manጋሽ

Meeting someone

The first minute

Names and origins come fast and warmly. Amarigna imaralehu — I am learning Amharic — is the sentence that turns any conversation into a free lesson.

simish man new?what is your name? (to a woman)ስምሽ ማን ነው?

What you will hear

Meant kindly — answer warmly

Numbers & the two clocks

Count — then confirm WHICH clock

Ethiopia counts hours from sunrise: one o-clock habesha time is 7 AM, noon is six. ALWAYS confirm habesha or ferenj se-at when arranging anything. This one note saves whole mornings.

assertenአስር
hayatwentyሃያ
hamsafiftyሃምሳ
metohundredመቶ

habesha se-atEthiopian timeሀበሻ ሰዓት

The minibus

How Ethiopia moves

Blue-white minibuses run fixed routes; the weyala hangs from the door chanting destinations and taking fares. To get off, shout weraj alle! In smaller towns, the bajaj rules.
taksiminibus taxiታክሲ
weyalathe conductorወያላ
weraj alle!getting off!ወራጅ አለ!
bajajthree-wheelerባጃጅ
wedetwhere to?ወዴት
qes belslow down (to a driver)ቀስ በል
qoywaitቆይ

Finding the way

Lost — briefly

Nobody leaves a lost visitor lost; expect an escort, not just directions. Open with yikirta, state tefahu, and let Ethiopian hospitality do the rest.

Eating

Injera is plate, fork and food

Everyone shares one injera, tearing with the RIGHT hand only. On Wednesdays and Fridays much of the menu goes fasting — vegan, ancient and excellent. Order either lane happily.

The coffee ceremony

Three rounds of friendship

Buna is roasted, ground and brewed in front of you, poured in three rounds — abol, tona, bereka. Leaving before the third is leaving early. A gursha — being fed by hand — is pure affection: accept it.

shayteaሻይ
jebenathe clay coffee potጀበና
iniblalet us eatእንብላ

betam tafach newit is very deliciousበጣም ጣፋጭ ነው
ameseginalehu, dehna hugnithank you, goodbye (to her)አመሰግናለሁ, ደህና ሁኚ

The gebeya & the suk

Where bargaining lives — and where it does not

Suk prices are fixed; gebeya prices are an opening bid. Ask sint new?, gasp betam wid new!, offer qenes — with a smile. The dance is expected and enjoyed.

sukcorner shopሱቅ

birrbirr (the currency)ብር
wid newit is expensiveውድ ነው
qenesreduce it! (bargaining)ቀንስ
rikash newit is cheapርካሽ ነው

When things go wrong

The rescue kit

Algebagnem plus ibakih digem rescue any conversation. For anything medical, hakim bet yet new? finds the clinic; medhanit is the medicine you leave with.

amemegnI am sickአመመኝ
hakim bet yet new?where is the clinic?ሐኪም ቤት የት ነው?
medhanitmedicineመድሃኒት
silkphoneስልክ

Leaving well

Blessings for the road

Common questions

How do you say hello in Amharic?

Selam. The greetings section above has the full set, including the reply you are likely to hear back.

Do I need an account to use these Amharic phrases?

No. Every phrase on this page is open to read, and you can hear all of them in Bhasaly without signing up.

Do I need to read the Amharic script to use these?

No. Every line is romanized first, so you can say it straight away. The script is there alongside if you want to start reading it.
