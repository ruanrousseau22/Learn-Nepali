First words

sain baina uu both ways

sain baina uu greets and asks how you are at once — answer sain and hand it straight back. zugeer smooths over everything; za closes any deal.

Being polite

What you will hear

At the ger door

At a ger you will hear orooroi — come in — before you can knock. tsai uugaarai puts milk tea in your hands; yavaarai sends you off well.
orooroicome in!Ороорой
tsai uugaaraihave some teaЦай уугаарай
chi tsai uukh uuwill you have tea?Чи цай уух уу?
khuukhedtei yuudo you have children?хүүхэдтэй юү?
za yavlaaright — I am off!За, явлаа
yavaaraisafe travels (the send-off)яваарай

In the ger

Clockwise, right hand, smile

Ger rules: walk in without knocking, move clockwise, never step on the threshold, receive everything with your RIGHT hand. The suutei tsai is salty — drink it anyway.

tom gera big gerтом гэр
suutei tsaimilk tea (salty!)сүүтэй цай
dailakhto host a mealдайлах
bolloodone · that is enoughболлоо
saikhanlovely · beautifulсайхан

Numbers

Tugrik in thousands

Prices run in myanga — thousands of tugrik. A bus fare is under myanga, buuz a few myanga each, and taxis charge by the kilometre.

Any car might stop

Directions

The compass does the work

baruun and zuun are right and left — and also west and east. Mongolians orient the world by compass; the ger door always faces south.
baruunright · westбаруун
zuunleft · eastзүүн
chigeereestraight aheadчигээрээ
oirkhonnearойрхон
kholfarхол

Eating

Meat is the menu

Buuz dumplings, khuushuur pastries, tsuivan noodles — meat carries the table. amttai! compliments the cook; vegetarians should learn makhgui, without meat.

At the zakh

Cash and a wince

The zakh is the everything-market. khyamd bolokh uu? asks nicely; unetei with a wince does half the work. Cash rules outside UB.
