Melmastia

The guest is sacred

melmastyaa — hospitality — is the Pashtun code, and you will be welcomed everywhere. The third cup of chaay is where refusing counts: bas, manana lands it kindly.

Eating

Ddoddey under everything

ddoddey is bread, food and the meal itself — warm naan under every dish. chaay means green unless you ask for tor; khwandawar praises the cook.

The bazaar

The expected dance

The bazaar expects the dance: dder graan day! then arzaan ye krra, both smiling. Settle the total before goods change hands.

Staying somewhere

Mind the false friend

A hottal is often an eatery — for sleeping, ask for a khaalee khuna. Power and water take evenings off, so pack a torch.

Getting help

Serious words, hopefully unused

mrasta wokrra! — help me — is the sentence to know; say it clearly. Keep your address written in Pashto; any shopkeeper will oblige.

Staying in touch

Phone & photos

SIMs are cheap with a passport. Cameras are a serious matter — anzur wakhlam? asks first, and with women and families the answer is usually no. Respect it.

Common questions

How do you say hello in Pashto?

Do I need an account to use these Pashto phrases?

No. Every phrase on this page is open to read, and you can hear all of them in Bhasaly without signing up.

Do I need to read the Pashto script to use these?

No. Every line is romanized first, so you can say it straight away. The script is there alongside if you want to start reading it.
