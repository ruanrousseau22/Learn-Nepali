First words

The ones you use every hour

namaste with palms together is hello AND goodbye, any time of day. Nepalis rarely say thank you aloud — save dhanyabaad for real favours — for everything else a smile and hunchha is enough.
namastehello · goodbyeनमस्ते
hajuryes? · pardon? (the polite everything-word)हजुर
hunchhaokay · sureहुन्छ
theek chhait is fineठीक छ
maaf garnussorry · excuse meमाफ गर्नुस्
hoyes (it is)हो
hoinano (it is not)होइन
dhanyabaadthank you (for real favours)धन्यवाद

Being polite

Respect lives in the verb — and in hajur

There is no everyday word for please — politeness rides on the -nus verb ending and on hajur. Called by name? Answer hajur. Strangers are family: daai and didi.
daaiolder brother — any man a bit olderदाइ
didiolder sister — any woman a bit olderदिदी
bhaaiyounger brotherभाइ
bahiniyounger sisterबहिनी
basnusplease sitबस्नुस्
parkhanusplease waitपर्खनुस्
hundainano, that will not workहुँदैन

Meeting someone

Introductions

mero naam … ho — my name is … — needs no verb changes. bhetera khusi laagyo — nice to meet you — is always warmly received.
mero naammy name (is…)मेरो नाम
tapaaiko naam ke ho?what is your name?तपाईंको नाम के हो?
bhetera khusi laagyonice to meet youभेटेर खुसी लाग्यो
ma alikati nepaali bolchhuI speak a little Nepaliम अलिकति नेपाली बोल्छु
ma bujhdinaI do not understandम बुझ्दिन
bistaarai bhannusplease say it slowlyबिस्तारै भन्नुस्
pheri bhetaunlaasee you againफेरि भेटौंला

What you will hear

Street greetings & replies

Two greetings double as small talk: kataa jaane? — where are you off to — and khaanaa khaanubhayo? — have you eaten? A vague cheerful answer is the right answer.

khaanaa khaanubhayo?have you eaten? (a greeting)खाना खानुभयो?

khaanuseat! (the invitation)खानुस्
kati basne?how long are you staying?कति बस्ने?
kun deshbaata?from which country?कुन देशबाट?

Taxis, buses & mountain roads

On the trail

Walking words

ukaalo is uphill, oraalo down — the two words that describe every day in the hills. bistaarai bistaarai — slowly, slowly — works as a complete sentence.

oraalodownhillओरालो

bistaaraislowlyबिस्तारै

Eating

Dal bhat & tea

daal bhaat comes with refills — the server returns until you say pugyo. thapnus asks for more, and khaanaa miTho chha makes the kitchen’s day.

Being a guest

In a Nepali home

Shoes come off at the door. Refusing food takes three warm tries — alikati maatra takes a small portion; pugyo plus a smile ends it kindly.
juttaa kholnusshoes off (you will be told)जुत्ता खोल्नुस्
alikati maatrajust a littleअलिकति मात्र
ghar raamro chhayour home is lovelyघर राम्रो छ
khaajaasnacks (you will be offered)खाजा
ma jaanchhu haiI will be off now (the polite leave-taking)म जान्छु है

Shopping

Fixed in shops, soft in markets

Shops have fixed prices; tourist markets bend a little. chaahidaina — I do not need it — turns away touts kindly. Keep walking as you say it.
mahango bhayothat is too expensiveमहँगो भयो
ali ghataaunusa little less, pleaseअलि घटाउनुस्
theek chha, linchhuokay, I will take itठीक छ, लिन्छु
chaahidainaI do not need itचाहिँदैन
herdai maatrajust lookingहेर्दै मात्र

At the hotel

Rooms & hot water

taato paani — hot water — is the question that matters, in city hotels and trail lodges alike. Ask for a kambal — a blanket — before the night turns cold.
ek raatko kati?how much for one night?एक रातको कति?
saanchothe keyसाँचो
chisocoldचिसो
kambalblanketकम्बल
bholi jaanchhuI leave tomorrowभोलि जान्छु

Getting help

Serious words, hopefully unused

maddat garnus — please help — is the sentence to know; say it clearly. Carry your address written down — any hotel will write it in Nepali for you.

Staying in touch

Phone, wifi & photos

Wifi is in most lodges and cafes — ask with the chha? frame. Local SIMs need your passport. Ask before photographing people, always.
