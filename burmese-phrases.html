First words

Add ba and it turns polite

mingalaba is the visitor’s hello — always right. Burmese goodbyes announce the leaving: thwa daw meh, I am off. The little ba on the end of anything makes it polite.
mingalabahelloမင်္ဂလာပါ
kyeizu tin ba dethank youကျေးဇူးတင်ပါတယ်
taun ban ba desorryတောင်းပန်ပါတယ်
houq kehyesဟုတ်ကဲ့
ma houq bunoမဟုတ်ဘူး
ya bigot it · OK nowရပြီ
keiq sa ma shi ba buno problemကိစ္စမရှိပါဘူး
thwa daw mehgoodbye — I am offသွားတော့မယ်

Being polite

The respectful address

hsaya — teacher — is the respectful address for any man skilled at his work: drivers, guides, waiters. A woman is hsayama. You will be told hpanaq chuq ba — please take off your shoes — at every doorstep.
hsayasir (any skilled man)ဆရာ
hsayamamadam · teacher (woman)ဆရာမ
hkana saun bawait a moment, pleaseခဏစောင့်ပါ
htain baplease sitထိုင်ပါ
la baplease comeလာပါ
hpanaq chuq bashoes off, please (you will be told)ဖိနပ်ချွတ်ပါ

Meeting someone

Introductions

Give your name plus ba and you have introduced yourself — the gendered I (men kyundaw, women kyama) can wait for the course.

beh ga la lehwhere are you from?ဘယ်ကလာလဲ
na ma leh buI do not understandနားမလည်ဘူး
pyan pyaw baplease say it againပြန်ပြောပါ
hpyei hpyei pyaw baplease speak slowlyဖြည်းဖြည်းပြောပါ

What you will hear

The eaten-yet greeting

htamin sa pi bi la? — eaten rice yet? — is a greeting, not lunch planning. sa pi bi answers it. In a teashop ba sa ma leh — what will you eat? — means the waiter is ready to take your order.
htamin sa pi bi laeaten yet? (a greeting)ထမင်းစားပြီးပြီလား
sa pi bialready eaten (the answer)စားပြီးပြီ
sa baeat! (the host insists)စားပါ
ba sa ma lehwhat will you eat?ဘာစားမလဲ
yauq piwe have arrivedရောက်ပြီ

Numbers

Kyat come in thousands

Tea is a few hundred kyat, a meal a few thousand, hotels in thaung and thein. Sellers will tap the number into a calculator and turn it round to you.

hse10ဆယ်
tiq ya100တစ်ရာ
nga ya500ငါးရာ
ta htaung1,000တစ်ထောင်
nga htaung kyaq5,000 kyatငါးထောင်ကျပ်
thaung10,000သောင်း
thein100,000သိန်း
kyaqkyatကျပ်

Getting around

No meters here

Yangon taxis have no meters — agree the fare through the window before you get in. hpyei hpyei asks the driver to slow down.
teq ka sitaxiတက္ကစီ
baq sa kabusဘတ်စ်ကား
kacarကား
hpyei hpyeislowly!ဖြည်းဖြည်း

The teashop

Myanmar’s living room

The teashop is where Myanmar sits: sweet milky lahpeq yeh, free refills of plain tea, mohinga for breakfast. taw bi stops the topping-up kindly.
lahpeq yeh hsaingthe teashopလက်ဖက်ရည်ဆိုင်
lahpeq yehsweet milk teaလက်ဖက်ရည်
kaw hpicoffeeကော်ဖီ
htaminrice · a mealထမင်း
ta hkweq pei baone cup, pleaseတစ်ခွက်ပေးပါ
saq deit is spicyစပ်တယ်
a yan kaung dereally good!အရမ်းကောင်းတယ်
taw bithat is enoughတော်ပြီ

At the zei

Bargain with a smile

shaw pei ba — please lower it — with a smile does the bargaining. Money changers want crisp, unfolded dollars; ma lo bu — no need — waves off persistent sellers.
zeithe marketဈေး
zei kyi deit is expensiveဈေးကြီးတယ်
zei paw deit is cheapဈေးပေါတယ်
shaw pei baplease lower the priceလျှော့ပေးပါ
ma lo buI do not need itမလိုဘူး
paiq hsanmoneyပိုက်ဆံ
paiq hsan leh deto change moneyပိုက်ဆံလဲတယ်

Staying somewhere

Rooms & torches

a hkan shi la — is there a room? — is the first question at the desk. Power cuts are routine everywhere — a phone torch is standard kit, not an emergency.
ho tehhotelဟိုတယ်
a hkanroomအခန်း
thawthe keyသော့

Getting help

Serious words, hopefully unused

ku nyi ba! — help, please — is the sentence to know; say it clearly. ne ma kaung bu tells a pharmacist how you feel; most speak some English.
ku nyi bahelp me, please!ကူညီပါ
yehpoliceရဲ
hsei younhospitalဆေးရုံ
hsaya wundoctorဆရာဝန်
hseimedicineဆေး
ne ma kaung buI am not feeling wellနေမကောင်းဘူး
thadi hta babe careful!သတိထားပါ

Staying in touch

Phone, wifi & photos

SIM cards are cheap with a passport. Wifi fades outside the cities — download your maps first. Ask before photographing people; a smile and a gesture do it.
hpounphoneဖုန်း
nan baqnumberနံပါတ်
hpoun nan baq beh lauq lehwhat is your number?ဖုန်းနံပါတ်ဘယ်လောက်လဲ
